# ADR 0007: Terrain (hills, valleys, a river with bridges, holes) and the starter map as a file

- Status: accepted, 2026-11-10
- Replaces, from ADR 0003:
  - "height: 0 everywhere for now" and "Drawing and walking on heights" in "Not now";
  - the starter world generated at boot from `STARTER_MAP_SEED`.
- Replaces, from ADR 0004: "Right drag, free, pans". A left drag pans now; the right click still cancels.

## Context

The world is flat grass with rocks. It needs relief and water to feel like a place: hills and valleys, a river, and
holes. Cells already store a height (a signed byte, always 0) and the chunk format has room for more ground kinds.

The starter world is generated by the server at boot. We want it as a file instead: changed by hand as the game
grows, the same for every server, and used again to reboot a world.

## Decisions

### What a cell holds

- **Ground kinds**, with their stored codes (format 1 of the chunks doesn't change):

  | Code | Kind     | Walkable | Notes                                   |
  |------|----------|----------|-----------------------------------------|
  | 0    | `void`   | no       | not drawn                               |
  | 1    | `grass`  | yes      |                                         |
  | 2    | `water`  | no       | the river                               |
  | 3    | `hole`   | no       | a pit, drawn                            |
  | 4    | `bridge` | yes      | a bridge's deck, over the river         |

- **Height**: −3 to +3. A map with a height outside is refused.
- **Objects**: rocks stand only on grass.

### Walking

- A cell is walkable when its ground is `grass` or `bridge` and no object stands on it.
- A **step** from a to b, straight or diagonal, needs b walkable and heights at most 1 apart. Two or more is a
  cliff.
- A **diagonal** step also needs both straight ways round (a → side → b) to be steps: no cutting the corner of a
  rock, a hole, the river or a cliff.
- Costs don't change: 1 straight, √2 diagonal, climbing included. Walking speed doesn't change.
- The areas the terrain numbers (ADR 0002) follow the same steps: two cells split by a cliff are in different areas
  when nothing else links them.
- Spawn stays the walkable cell closest to the center.
- Clicking water or a hole works like clicking a rock: the hover shows, the menu opens, and Move is off.

### The generated world

The generator makes the starter world, 1024 × 512 and wrapping, from a seed. The same seed gives the same world.

- **Relief**: smooth noise that wraps both ways, cut into levels:
  - about half the cells at 0, a quarter above, a quarter below;
  - hills and valleys are soft blobs 20 to 80 cells across;
  - levels 3 and −3 are rare, about 2% of cells each.
- **No cliffs**: any two neighbors, diagonals included, are at most 1 apart. Where they aren't, the one farther from 0
  (both on a tie) is moved one level toward 0, pass after pass, until none is left. Water never moves.
- **The river**:
  - one loop around the ring: every column has water, and the water of a column touches the next one's;
  - its center row wobbles smoothly by up to ±40 rows, and its width goes slowly from 3 to 6 cells;
  - at the spawn's column, its edge is 15 to 30 cells from the spawn, so you see it soon after joining;
  - water is at −1 all the way round. Cells near it are pulled toward that level: a cell k cells from the water
    (counting diagonals as 1) is kept between −k and k − 1, so banks are at −1 or 0 and the river cuts through
    hills in terraces;
  - it flows east (columns increasing).
- **Bridges**:
  - about 10, one every ~100 columns, the first within 30 columns of the spawn;
  - each is 1 column wide and spans the river from bank to bank, so its cell is `bridge` at height 0;
  - the bank cell at each of its ends is set to 0, and no rock stands there.
- **Holes**:
  - about one every 2 to 3 chunks: blobs of 1 to 6 cells;
  - at least 3 cells from water and bridges, and never under a rock;
  - a hole cell keeps its height.
- **Rocks**: about 12% of the grass cells, never on a bridge's end cells.
- **Regions**: as before (ADR 0003), 12 points and the closest one wins. They don't shape the terrain.

### The starter map file

- `back/mapfiles/world/` holds the starter world:
  - `map.json`:

    ```json
    {"format": 1, "slug": "world", "name": "World", "width": 1024, "height": 512, "wraps": true,
     "regions": [{"id": 1, "name": "Sprinkle Meadow", "biome": "meadow", "color": "#7fd99a"}]}
    ```

    Each region's color is how it's painted in `regions.png`. Colors are distinct.
  - four PNGs at the map's size, 1 pixel per cell, column 0 on the left and row 0 at the top:

    | File          | Holds                                                                               |
    |---------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
    | `ground.png`  | transparent `void`, grass `#74d3a5`, water `#6fb7e0`, hole `#2b2540`, bridge `#b98a5a` |
    | `height.png`  | gray levels: −3 `#202020`, −2 `#404040`, −1 `#606060`, 0 `#808080`, 1 `#a0a0a0`, 2 `#c0c0c0`, 3 `#e0e0e0` |
    | `regions.png` | transparent for none, else the region's color from `map.json`                        |
    | `objects.png` | transparent for nothing, rock `#d2cec5`                                              |

  - `README.md` lists these colors and how to edit them. `palette.gpl` holds them for GIMP and Aseprite.
- The folder is embedded in the server binary, so every server ships with it.
- **Loading** checks what the game needs, and refuses the whole file at the first problem, naming it:
  - `map.json` reads, its format is 1, and the map's own rules hold (size, wrapping, regions);
  - each PNG has the map's size, and every pixel is fully transparent or fully opaque with a known color. For
    example, `height.png (412, 87): #8c8c8c isn't a height`;
  - rocks stand only on grass.

  It doesn't check the generator's own rules (no cliffs, holes away from water): hand-made cliffs are fine.
- Edits are by hand in any image editor, or by running the generator again.

### Commands

- `make world`: runs the generator (`go run ./cmd/mapfile generate -seed 42 -out mapfiles/world`). It refuses to
  write into a folder that has files unless `-force` is given (`make world FORCE=1`), and then it rewrites every file
  of the map folder. Hand edits are lost; git keeps them.
- `make check-world`: `go run ./cmd/mapfile check mapfiles/world`, the loading checks, without a database. A test
  runs them on the committed folder too, so a bad file never ships.
- `make reset-world`: `go run ./cmd/donut reset-map world`, with the server stopped. It replaces the map `world` in
  the database with the embedded file, in one transaction: its chunks, regions and rocks. Rocks get new ids.

### The server

- At boot with no map in the database, it loads the embedded `world` and saves it, as it saved the generated one.
- It never generates a map. `STARTER_MAP_SEED` is gone: the seed is the generator's `-seed`.
- A changed file never replaces a world on its own: only `reset-map` does.
- **Migration `0003`** deletes every map, so the next boot loads the file.

### Drawing

- **Levels**: each level up draws a cell 8 art pixels higher. A cell's drawn ground center is its flat one, 8 px × its
  height higher.
- **Cliff faces**: a cell higher than its front neighbor (+column or +row) shows a face on that side, down to the
  neighbor's height. It goes down to −3 when the neighbor is `void` or in a chunk not held, so the edge of what you
  hold looks like a slab.
  - The face's look depends on the cell: earth for grass and holes, wood posts for bridges.
  - The +row side faces the light (top-left) and is lighter than the +column side.
  - An earth face is warm brown, with a darker band at the bottom of each level.
- **Layers**, from bottom to top:
  1. **Water**: one sprite per water cell, at its height. It sits under the ground, which is transparent on water
     cells, so a bank in front hides the water behind it.
  2. **Ground**: one texture per chunk, as before, now with heights and faces.
  3. **Overlay**: the hover and selection borders, and the path line.
  4. **Entities**: rocks and players.
- **Water**:
  - pastel blue in 2 or 3 shades;
  - it flows: 4 frames of 250 ms each, every water cell on the same frame. Light streaks move a quarter of a cell
    east per frame, so the loop is seamless;
  - a lighter foam line on each edge next to a cell that isn't water.
- **Terrace rims**: a grass top has a line of the earth faces' brown, one pixel wide, along each edge toward a lower
  cell, or one that is `void` or not held, like faces: the lit brown on its left edges (nw, sw), the darker one on its
  right edges (ne, se). It marks the top of every step, the back edges included, which show no face: the grass on
  both levels is the same green. A corner whose diagonal neighbor is lower gets its 2 pixels too: at an inner corner
  of a contour, the rims of two cells meet on a third cell's corner, which would leave a gap.
- **Holes**: a dark pit, its inner walls earth fading to near black, with no bottom visible. Each edge next to a cell
  that isn't a hole has a thin darker rim.
- **Bridges**: warm wood planks laid across the river (along the rows), with a low rail on each side.
- **Neighbors across chunks**: faces, foam and rims depend on neighbors, which can be in another chunk. Before
  that chunk is held, a neighbor counts as `void` for faces and as the same kind for foam and rims. Chunks are drawn
  on the next frame: the ones that arrived, then the held chunks beside them (4 sides), each once. A forgotten
  chunk's neighbors aren't drawn again: they're at the far edge of what you hold.
- **On the heights**: rocks, players, the hover and selection borders, the action menu, and each point of the path
  line are drawn at their cell's height. A cell in a chunk not held counts as 0. A step between levels glides
  straight, rising or dropping during the step. Players are shown before their chunk arrives: when it does, each
  glide goes on to the right height in the time it had left.
- **Picking** gives the topmost cell under the pointer. A cell shows as a column, from its top down to −3: its faces,
  and below them what the cells in front hide. Going down from level 3 to −3 a quarter of a pixel at a time, it takes
  the cell whose ground at that level holds the pointer. The first one whose height is at least that level wins: its
  top, or its face, is what's drawn there. Whole levels only would miss faces, which span between them. A cell in a
  chunk not held counts as 0.
- Rocks and players stand above all ground. A rock or player just behind a raised cell isn't hidden by it, which is
  wrong by up to 8 px at their feet. Correct occlusion waits for taller art (`docs/FOR_LATER.md`).

### Panning with the left button

On a trackpad, a right drag is a two-finger drag. So:

- **A left drag pans**, free: the world follows the pointer, as the right drag did (ADR 0004). Locked, it does
  nothing, and a drag still never unlocks the camera.
- It starts on the world only: a drag from the action menu or the camera icon doesn't pan.
- **A left click** still selects a tile, or cancels outside the map. The release of a drag is no click: under 4 px
  of movement between press and release, it's a click, as for the right button.
- **A right click** still cancels. A right drag does nothing.

### The map screen

- The image (ADR 0005) gets the terrain: water `#6fb7e0`, holes `#2b2540`, bridges `#b98a5a`.
- Grass is shaded by height, on top of its region's shade and border: lighter going up, darker going down, about 7%
  per level.
- It stays a paletted PNG: 13 shades × 2 (border or not) × 7 levels, plus water, holes, bridges, rocks and
  transparent, make 187 colors.

### Websocket

- `chunk` keeps its shape. `kinds` may now hold `water`, `hole` and `bridge`.
- The client drops a chunk with a height outside −3…3.

### Logs

Debug logs for:

- the generator: the time it took, the cells per level and per ground kind, the rocks, and the spawn with the
  river's rows at its column;
- loading a map file: its size, regions and rocks, and the time it took. A refused file logs the problem;
- `reset-map`: the map deleted and the one saved;
- the client: chunks drawn again because a neighbor arrived, with timings.

### How it is built

- Back:
  - `worldmap/domain`:
    - `map.go`: the new kinds, the height range, and rocks only on grass;
    - `terrain.go` and `path.go`: walkable kinds, steps on heights, and areas flooded by steps;
    - `generate.go` is split into one file per step: `relief.go`, `river.go`, `bridges.go`, `holes.go`;
    - `image.go`: terrain colors and height shading.
  - `worldmap/infra/mapfile.go`: reading and writing the map folder, and its checks. It works on any `fs.FS`.
  - `worldmap/app`: `EnsureStarterMap` loads the file instead of generating it, and `ResetMap` replaces a map.
    The repo deletes a map by slug.
  - `mapfiles/`: the `world` folder, and `embed.go`.
  - `cmd/mapfile`: `generate` and `check`.
  - `cmd/donut`: the `reset-map` subcommand, and `STARTER_MAP_SEED` gone.
  - `migrations/up/0003.up.sql`.
- Client:
  - `map/worldMap.ts`: the new kinds. `map/chunks.ts`: a cell's height.
  - `net/protocol.ts`: heights within −3…3.
  - `iso/heights.ts`: a cell's drawn position at its height, and picking on heights.
  - `map/chunks.ts`: `HeldChunksRef`, the chunks held, which the session keeps and the views read.
  - `art/`: `edges.ts` (a diamond's edges), `cliff.ts`, `water.ts`, `hole.ts`, `bridge.ts`.
  - `render/tileGraphics.ts`: the terrain's sprites, painted on first use and kept by their edges.
  - `render/chunkView.ts`: faces and the neighbors they need, the water layer, and drawing again when a neighbor
    arrives.
  - `render/playersView.ts`, `render/overlay.ts`, `render/glide.ts`: drawing at heights.
  - `render/mapView.ts`: the water layer under the ground.

### Security review

- Checked:
  - The map file is built into the binary and trusted, but still checked in full before use. `map.json`'s size is
    checked first (at most 4096 × 4096), and each PNG's declared size is read before it's decoded and must be the
    map's: no layer is decoded before its size is known to be right.
  - Every pixel value read becomes a ground code, a level or a region only through the layer's own colors, so no
    index can go out of range. Region colors are parsed strictly and must be distinct.
  - `mapfiles.Read` takes a slug from the command line: `fs.Sub` refuses `..` and other invalid paths (tested), and
    only folders built into the binary can be read.
  - `reset-map` runs only from a shell, with the database URL, never over HTTP. Its SQL is parameterized, and the
    delete and the insert run in one transaction: a refused file or a failed insert leaves the old map (tested).
  - `mapfile generate` writes only the map folder's fixed file names into the folder given, and refuses a folder
    with files unless `-force` is given. `mapfile check` only reads.
  - The client drops a chunk with an unknown kind or a height outside −3…3. The terrain's sprites are kept by their
    edges and frame only, a hundred at most, so nothing the server sends grows them. Water sprites share these
    textures: a chunk's water is destroyed without them, and its ground texture with it.
  - Paths on heights check a few more neighbors per step, under the same 8,192-cell search limit.
- Known risks:
  - `mapfile generate -force` follows a symbolic link named like one of the folder's files. It's a developer's tool
    run on their own checkout.
  - Picking scans up to 192 levels per pointer move and per frame: cheap, but it grows with the level range.

### Not now

- Correct occlusion of rocks and players behind raised cells (`docs/FOR_LATER.md`).
- Hand edits that survive a new run of the generator: overlay files over the generated map (`docs/FOR_LATER.md`).
- Lakes, chasms, stairs, waterfalls.
- Building or breaking bridges: a live chunk change (ADR 0003).
- Terrain shaped by regions and biomes.

## Test cases

### Back: map rules (`worldmap/domain`)

1. A map is refused when:
   - a height is outside −3…3;
   - a rock stands on water, a hole or a bridge.

   The new ground codes 2, 3 and 4 are accepted.

### Back: walking on terrain (`worldmap/domain`)

2. Walkable: grass and bridge. Not walkable: water, hole, void, and any cell with a rock.
3. Steps:
   - from 0 to 1, and from 1 to 0, is a step;
   - from 0 to 2 isn't, either way;
   - a diagonal from 0 to 1 is refused when one side cell is at 2, a hole, or water.
4. Paths:
   - a hill of terraces (0, 1, 2, 3) is climbed ring by ring;
   - a plateau at 2 surrounded by cells at 0 can't be reached, and gets an empty path;
   - a path crosses the river only over a bridge.
5. Areas: two halves split by a cliff line are two areas; a ramp of steps joins them into one.
6. On random maps with heights, wrapping or not: every step is a walkable neighbor at most 1 level apart, no corner
   is cut, and the cost equals a brute-force shortest cost.

### Back: the generator (`worldmap/domain`)

7. The same seed gives the same map, and another seed another one.
8. Relief:
   - every height is within −3…3;
   - any two neighbors, diagonals and across the seams included, are at most 1 apart;
   - 40 to 60% of the cells are at 0;
   - some cells are at 3 and some at −3, each under 5%.
9. The river:
   - every column has water;
   - the water of each column touches the next one's (straight or diagonal), and column 1023's touches column 0's;
   - each column's water is 3 to 6 cells, in one run of rows;
   - every water cell is at −1, and every cell next to water is at −1 or 0;
   - at the spawn's column, the water is 15 to 30 cells from the spawn.
10. Bridges:
    - 8 to 12 bridges, each 1 column of `bridge` cells at 0, spanning the river from bank to bank;
    - one is within 30 columns of the spawn;
    - the end cells are at 0 and walkable;
    - from the spawn, a path reaches the cell just past the far end of the nearest bridge.
11. Holes: some exist, in blobs of 1 to 6 cells, each at least 3 cells from any water or bridge.
12. Rocks only on grass, never on a bridge's end cells.
13. The generated world passes the map file's checks.

### Back: the map file (`worldmap/infra`)

14. A map written to a folder reads back the same: ground, heights, regions, rocks, name, size and wrapping.
15. Loading refuses, naming the file and the pixel:
    - an unknown format in `map.json`;
    - a PNG of the wrong size;
    - an unknown color, or a half-transparent pixel, in each PNG;
    - a color in `regions.png` that isn't a region's;
    - two regions with the same color;
    - a rock on water.
16. A hand-made cliff (0 next to 2) loads.
17. The committed `mapfiles/world` passes every check.

### Back: commands and boot (`cmd/mapfile`, `cmd/donut`, `worldmap/app`)

18. `generate` refuses a folder that has files without `-force`, and rewrites it with `-force`.
19. `check` exits non-zero on a bad folder, naming the problem.
20. On an empty database, boot saves the embedded world: 512 chunks, 12 regions, its rocks. A second boot saves
    nothing.
21. `reset-map world` replaces the world: its chunks, regions and rocks are the file's, and none of the old ones
    remain.
22. Migration `0003` deletes every map.

### Back: the map image (`worldmap/domain`)

23. Water, holes and bridges have their colors.
24. Grass at height 2 is lighter than at 0, which is lighter than at −2, in the same region.
25. The image stays a paletted PNG.

### Client

26. Protocol: a `chunk` with `water`, `hole` and `bridge` decodes. A height of 4 or −4 drops it.
27. Chunks: a cell's height comes from its chunk; a cell not held has none.
28. Drawn position: a cell at height 2 is drawn 16 px higher than at 0.
29. Picking:
    - over a flat area, the cell under the pointer, as before;
    - over a cell at 2, its top picks it, not the cell its flat position would give;
    - over the face of a raised cell, that cell;
    - a raised cell in front hides the cell behind it.
30. Faces:
    - a cell at 1 next to a front neighbor at 0 has a face 1 level high on that side;
    - no face toward a neighbor at the same height or higher, or toward a cell behind;
    - toward `void` or a chunk not held, the face goes down to −3.
31. The ground leaves water cells out, and the water layer has one sprite per water cell, at its height.
32. Water frames: the frame changes every 250 ms and loops after 4.
33. Foam: a water cell gets foam on the edges next to non-water cells, not on edges next to water, nor next to a chunk
    not held.
34. Hole rims: on the edges next to non-hole cells only.
35. A chunk arriving draws again the held chunks on its 4 sides, once each, even when several arrive in the same
    frame.
36. Players, rocks, borders and the path line are drawn at their cell's height. A step from 0 to 1 glides from
    one height to the other.
37. Terrace rims: a grass top has a rim on its edges toward a lower cell, back edges included, and toward void or a
    chunk not held; none toward a cell as high or higher. At an inner corner, the cell whose diagonal neighbor is
    lower gets that corner, and a corner's rim is its 2 pixels.
38. Panning: a left drag pans and a right drag does nothing; the release of a left drag is no click, even back
    where it started, and a press and release within 4 px is one.
